Monthly Business Review — July 2026

Leaders: Tom Boettner, Liz Mackie & Alex Congleton  |  Domain: Fantasy

📝 Executive Summary

July was the month the CAT dependencies stopped being "done" and became Released. Universal Wallet (FEAT-8216), Payments MVP (FEAT-8217), and Geolocation (FEAT-8511) all moved from Ready for Promotion to Released on July 31, and Trading Engineering Alpha (FEAT-8554) reached Released on July 24. Event Page (FEAT-8853) — the Red that opened the June MBR — advanced to Ready for Product Review on July 30. App Switcher (FEAT-8600) was Abandoned on July 30 as that concept was rescoped.

On the DFS side the team closed 164 stories, bugs and tasks (146.5 SP) across M31.1 and M31.2, and 11 FNTSY epics reached Done. Player Cards came together end-to-end (new GetPlayerCards BFF endpoint, expanded list view, doubles markets), the pick-slip gained realtime offers and PBT promo chips, Playmaker picked up full market-configuration management, and Fast Lane added post-deploy tests in Prod plus documented E2E user journeys.

The concern is throughput, and it is now a trend rather than a blip. M31.2 closed at 29% SP completion (50.0 of 170.0) — below M30.4's 34% and M31.1's 51%. Sprint scope grew over the same window (157 → 208 items), so the gap between committed and completed is widening, not closing. Bugs were 40% of everything closed (65 of 164). Two dependencies remain open against the Beta window: RG & Entry Limits (FEAT-8509, unchanged since July 21) and Trading Engineering Beta (FEAT-8902). Epic closure also slowed markedly — 11 in July against 22 in June. M31.3 (Jul 23 – Aug 4) was still in flight at the time of writing.

Three CAT Features Reached Released — Not Just "Done"
June closed the CAT dependency wall at Ready for Promotion. July got them the rest of the way:
  • Universal Wallet (FEAT-8216) — Released, July 31
  • Payments MVP (FEAT-8217) — Released, July 31
  • Geolocation (FEAT-8511) — Released, July 31

OddsFactory's Alpha requirements reached Released on July 24, closing the track that went dev-complete in June. The domain is consuming live trading data end-to-end. Beta (FEAT-8902) remains In Progress and is now the forward-looking OF dependency.

FEAT-8853 was flagged Red in the June MBR. By July 30 it had advanced to Ready for Product Review — i.e. ready for UAT. Jasmine Dacones carried the Event Page screen, CMS entry and navigation work; Anushri Patel's player-card list view landed on the same surface. The June Red is materially retired.

Anushri Patel closed the highest story-point load on the team (13 items, 22.0 SP): switching player cards to the new GetPlayerCards BFF endpoint, the expanded list view with last-5 detail and alt-lines, doubles markets, More/Less pick buttons, line/market display, and CMS configuration. Fast Lane Reaches Prod Post-Deploy Testing
Patrick Kilburn moved launch-readiness plumbing from Cert into Prod: post-deploy tests for fantasy-jobs-worker and playmaker-fantasy-api in Prod, plus defined and documented E2E user journeys. ⚠️ Challenges & Risks

M31.2 Closed at 29% — Third Consecutive Sprint Under 55%
The trend, not the single data point, is the issue:
  • M30.3 — 81.7 of 134.3 SP (61%)
  • M30.4 — 58.5 of 171.0 SP (34%)
  • M31.1 — 96.5 of 187.5 SP (51%)
  • M31.2 — 50.0 of 170.0 SP (29%)
Sprint scope grew across the same window (157 → 208 items in M31.1 → M31.2) while completed SP nearly halved. June's action item was to rebaseline M31.1 against realistic FE capacity; M31.2's result suggests that did not happen, or did not hold.
📌 Action Item: Treat committed scope as a hard constraint for M31.3/M31.4 rather than an aspiration. Establish the actual 4-sprint rolling completion rate (≈43% by SP) and plan the Beta window against that number, not against capacity.
Bugs Were 40% of Everything Closed
65 of the 164 items closed in July were Bugs, against 99 Stories — and 123 bugs sat in the two sprints in total. A rising bug share this close to a launch window competes directly with the remaining feature scope, and partly explains the SP completion rate: bug fixes consume sprint capacity while carrying few or no story points.
📌 Action Item: Separate bug capacity from feature capacity in M31.3+ planning so the feature burn-down reflects reality.
Two Dependencies Still Open Against the Beta Window
  • RG & Entry Limits (FEAT-8509) — still In Progress, last updated July 21. It was Amber in June on the basis that NATS UI work was scheduled into M31.1; that work has not closed, and the item has been static for two weeks.
  • Trading Engineering Beta (FEAT-8902) — In Progress, last updated July 24. Alpha is Released; Beta is the remaining OF commitment for M31.
These are the only two live cross-team risks left, which is a much better position than April — but both are on the Beta critical path and neither moved in the back half of July.
Epic Closure Slowed: 11 vs 22 in June
June retired most of the backend MVP spine (22 epics). July closed 11, and none of them after July 22. Some of this is expected — the big BE epics were already closed — but combined with the falling SP completion rate it suggests work is being carried rather than finished.
Dependency Tracker Is Stale
config/dependencies.yaml — the source for the Dependencies dashboard — still lists FEAT-8216, FEAT-8217 and FEAT-8511 as In Progress when Jira has all three as Released, and it has no entry for FEAT-8865 or FEAT-8853. Its weekly notes were last touched on 6/11. The table below was rebuilt from live Jira rather than from the tracker.
📌 Action Item: Refresh dependencies.yaml statuses and notes, and add the two missing features, so the Dependencies page stops disagreeing with Jira.
Metrics Telemetry Gap: July 23–28
An abrupt host power loss on July 22 at 13:47 truncated the metrics database mid-write and the collection pipeline was down until July 29 — a six-day hole (Jul 23–28) in sprint_snapshots and ticket_status_history. The database was recovered with no row loss, and the numbers in this MBR are unaffected: both M31.1 and M31.2 closed before the gap, and the epic count is taken from current Jira state rather than transition history. What is affected is in-sprint trend data for M31.3 across that window, and any burn-up chart spanning it. Durability settings have since been hardened.
